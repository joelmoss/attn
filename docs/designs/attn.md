## Problem Statement

Joel never opens macOS Notification Centre. It always holds dozens of notifications, most of them useless or already seen. Notifications have no distinction between "needs me to act" and "just so you know", no severity, and no count of unread items. Outside the transient banner there's no other signal. He wants severity, a clear action-vs-notice split, an unread count, and iOS-style live alerts in the Dock and menu bar. His first question was whether the built-in system can be changed. It can't, so this is a companion app that becomes the place he looks.

## What Makes This Cool

The Mac shows what your tools are doing right now. Joel's words: "I want to see iOS-style live activities in the dock, but not as a badge." The Dock icon becomes a live ring. Each segment is one activity: outlined while running, solid when it needs you, dashed when the sender has gone quiet. One look tells you whether something needs you and how urgently; the menu bar panel tells you which (eng review D12).

## Constraints (verified)

- **Notification Centre can't be modified or replaced.** There's no public API to restyle it, label others' notifications, or read them ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/758451)).
- **No interception.** Apps hand notifications to `usernoted` over XPC, Apple's private messaging between processes. Sitting in that path means injecting into a platform process with System Integrity Protection (SIP) off. Rejected: it weakens security across the whole Mac and breaks on updates.
- **The store needs Full Disk Access.** It lives at `~/Library/Group Containers/group.com.apple.usernoted/db2/db`. On this Mac (macOS 27.0.1), a read-only open outside the sandbox returned `authorization denied`. The `record` table has `app_id`, `delivered_date`, `presented` and `data`, a binary plist holding `req.titl/subt/body` ([plaso](https://plaso.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_modules/plaso/parsers/sqlite_plugins/macos_notification_center.html), [Objective-See](https://objective-see.org/blog/blog_0x2E.html)).
- **The Dock can't be extended, but your own icon is a canvas.** `NSDockTile.contentView` takes a custom view, redrawn with `display()`. Chromium limits it to about 5 redraws a second ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsdocktile/contentview.md), [Chromium](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/125.0.6422.5/chrome/browser/ui/cocoa/dock_icon.mm)). It's one square tile, drawn as snapshots, and only while the app runs. Animated subviews are [unreliable](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765451).
- **Mac Live Activities only mirror the iPhone's.** Mac apps can't post their own ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120684)).
- **Apple's own option.** Per Apple's macOS 27 help, Apple Intelligence can summarize notifications and rank them by priority ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-gw/guide/mac-help/mchldf5e4cb6)). It doesn't give a count, an action-vs-notice split, or live status.

## Premises (agreed)

1. **Companion, not replacement.** Notification Centre stays. *(Store reading and banner switch-over are follow-up scope, CEO D4.)* *Revised after the second opinion:* banners only go off per app, once that app's notifications are confirmed arriving in the tool. A loud warning shows if reading the store breaks after a macOS update.
2. **Two inputs, one activity store.** (a) *Follow-up (CEO D4):* Apple's notification store: whatever the app put in the record, but no urgency, expiry or resolution. (b) A direct sender channel carrying severity, expiry, ongoing state, "resolved" and an exact target to open.
3. **Agents and GitHub use (b).** v1: Claude Code hooks (CEO D1) and the GitHub notifications feed (CEO D5). Follow-ups: Codex's notify program (CEO D6), EventKit calendar (eng D2), chat DMs via (a) plus AI (CEO D4).
4. **"Live" means our own surfaces.** A live Dock tile (not a badge), a fixed-width menu bar item, and a floating live pill under the notch in v1 (CEO D7). Not Apple's Live Activities API.
5. **On-device AI labelling** *(follow-up with the store, CEO D4)*. It uses Foundation Models because notification text is private, and learns from your corrections. Labels the sender sets always win.
6. **Useful for anyone, not just Joel** (user, eng review 2026-10-28). Distribution: signed, notarized, Homebrew cask, Sparkle (eng D6). B needs Full Disk Access and no sandbox, so no Mac App Store for B.

## Cross-Model Perspective (Codex, gpt-6.1-sol)

- **Coolest version:** one persistent activity per piece of work. "Agent finished → CI failed → needs your decision" is one line, not four notifications. Track *activity state* and *attention* separately. Opening an item acknowledges the attention while the work can stay visibly running, and a new request raises attention again.
- **Most revealing answer:** "I want to see iOS-style live activities in the dock, but not as a badge." The live Dock is the reason to build this, and sorting supports it.
- **50% already exists:** [Nonja](https://github.com/piro0919/nonja) (MIT, Swift; repo, LICENSE and `Sources/Store.swift`, `Opener.swift` and `Engine.swift` verified to exist). It reads the store into a menu bar list. The other 50% is the activity model, the sender protocol, the live Dock tile, and correlation/classification.
- **Findings carried into this design:**
  - *High:* the store is an unsupported dependency. That led to the revised premise 1.
  - *High:* Accessibility click-through is best effort. Senders on the direct channel must supply an exact open target.
  - *Medium:* check model availability and keep a fallback for AI labels.
  - *Low:* the store does carry identity and time, just no attention or resolution contract.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Direct channel first (CHOSEN)
`attn` CLI → Unix socket → activity store → menu bar panel + live `NSDockTile`. The app is standalone and unrelated to any other app (user, eng review 2026-10-28). v1 ships two built-in senders: Claude Code hooks installed by `attn setup claude` (CEO D1) and the GitHub notifications feed (CEO D5), plus the generic `attn` CLI for any app. v1 surfaces: Dock ring, menu bar panel, floating live pill (CEO D7), own banner on new attention (CEO D3), global hotkey (CEO D8) and snooze (CEO D10). Follow-ups: Notification Centre store + AI triage (CEO D4), Codex (CEO D6), Focus-aware attention (CEO D9), EventKit (eng D2). No Full Disk Access, no store reading, no AI. Effort re-estimated after the CEO review: L (human ~4 weeks / CC ~3 days) [estimated], including the release pipeline (eng D6). Completeness 6/10: chat stays in Notification Centre.

### Approach B: Full two-input companion
A plus a store adapter (modelled on Nonja's `Store.swift`) acting as one more sender. Also: per-app banner switch-over with a health warning, Foundation Models labelling with stored corrections, and best-effort click-through. Effort XL (human ~6-8 weeks / CC ~1 week). This is the trajectory after A.

### Approach C: Work, not notifications
One activity per PR or workspace, grouping agent → CI → review → deploy. Effort L. Kept as a later layer on A's channel. Grouping needs shared IDs that only some senders provide.

## Recommended Approach

**A first, growing into B.** It delivers the live Dock and attention experience on data you control. Every Q3 clearing rule works there: clicking acknowledges, the source can resolve, and the sender can set an expiry. B's store adapter and AI then plug in as one more sender, without changing the activity model.

Decision logged: `70cad826-3d44-440b-aa8d-0b8be891c26d`. Ceiling: third-party app notifications (chat) stay in Notification Centre. Upgrade to B once A is in daily use and the store probe (Next Steps #6) confirms which apps land in the store.

**v1 behaviour details (CEO review spec pass, 2026-10-29; clarifies approved CEO D1, D3, D5, D7, D8, D10):**
- **Banner (D3):** an attention episode starts whenever attention rises under the D9 rule. One banner per item episode, only when the panel isn't open; non-critical banners are held 2 s, then posted individually, or as one summary banner ("3 items need you") when 3 or more items gained attention in that window; critical banners post at once (CEO D15). When the hold ends, the app re-checks attention and whether the panel is open, and drops the banner if the item was acked, snoozed or resolved in the meantime. Clicking a banner = Open on that item (D10 rules); clicking a summary opens the panel. Critical items request the time-sensitive interruption level (entitlement and behaviour checked in the spike). When an item is acked, resolved or dismissed, its delivered banner is removed from Notification Centre, so the app adds nothing to that pile. If notification permission is denied, the ring, pill and panel still work, and the panel shows one line: "Banners off — enable in System Settings".
- **Claude Code hooks (D1):** item id = Claude Code session id. `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse` and `PostToolUse` push `running`; `Notification` pushes `waiting` only for notification types on an allowlist of real requests for input or permission (other types such as successful auth or answered elicitations are ignored; the allowlist is checked against Claude Code's hook docs in the spike, CEO outside voice #3), and is ignored when the item is `done`, so the idle reminder after `Stop` doesn't raise attention; `Stop` pushes `done`; `StopFailure` pushes `error` with attention and severity high (re-review D5); `SessionEnd` pushes `resolve`, so a closed session never leaves a stale Needs you item (a session killed without hooks still needs Dismiss). Every hook push carries `--revision auto`: wall-clock nanoseconds taken when `attn` starts. Auto revisions only order pushes: they never raise attention by themselves (only a state transition, or an explicit `--revision` from a sender, does), so repeated `Notification` pushes don't re-raise an acked or snoozed item. A push is dropped only if its revision is strictly below the stored one; equal revisions apply in arrival order. The spike also checks that a still-running tool or subagent hook can't clear an unanswered permission prompt. Between two permission prompts the tool runs, so the second prompt is a transition and raises attention again; this ordering is verified in the spike. Hooks call `attn hook claude`, a thin forwarder (eng re-review D1): it stamps the auto revision and forwards only `session_id`, `agent_id`, `hook_event_name`, `notification_type`, `message` (Notification only), `cwd` and `TERM_PROGRAM`, capped at 16 KB; tool events carrying an `agent_id` (subagents) refresh the heartbeat only and never clear the main session's waiting (re-review D3), so prompts and tool input/output never leave the CLI (re-review D2); the app owns the Notification-type allowlist and the state mapping. It always exits 0 silently (even when the app isn't running), and bounds its whole run, socket included, to 200 ms, so a hung app can't stall Claude (tested against an app that accepts connections but stops answering; CEO outside voice #4); Claude Code treats exit 2 as blocking (verify in the spike). Agent items' `open` = the terminal app from `TERM_PROGRAM` via a fixed map (`Apple_Terminal` → Terminal, `iTerm.app` → iTerm, `ghostty` → Ghostty, `vscode` → VS Code, `WarpTerminal` → Warp), resolved to the installed app with `NSWorkspace.urlForApplication(withBundleIdentifier:)` and opened as a `file://` app URL; unknown or missing terminals get no `open`, and Open just acks. `attn setup claude` writes the absolute path of the `attn` binary inside the app bundle (`/Applications/<App>.app/Contents/MacOS/attn`), and the app re-checks that path at launch and offers to update the hooks if the app has moved; is idempotent (recognises its own entries), refuses a settings.json it can't parse, shows the diff and asks before writing; `attn setup claude --remove` removes its entries.
- **GitHub feed (D5):** item id = notification thread id; revision = the thread's `updated_at`, so a new mention or review request on an acked thread raises attention again. `review_requested`, `mention` and `assign` → `waiting` + attention (severity normal). `ci_activity` → `error` + attention (severity high) only when the run failed; a non-failed CI thread goes to Recent with no attention, and resolves when it leaves the unread list; the conclusion is fetched for each new or updated CI thread, and a failed lookup is persisted and retried every poll, independent of thread updates and 304 responses, until it succeeds (whether the reason also covers successful runs is verified in the spike). `open` = the subject's web link; if none can be resolved (e.g. a check suite), the PR's or repository's `html_url`. Resolve = the thread leaves the unread list (read or marked done on GitHub), detected by diffing against the previous set only after a complete, successful, paginated read; a 304, a partial page set or an error keeps the previous set unchanged (CEO outside voice #2). There is no separate PR-closed check. First run: unread threads older than the app's first launch go to Recent quietly, with no attention or banners. `gh` lookup: `/opt/homebrew/bin`, `/usr/local/bin`, then the login shell's `command -v gh`, or a path set in Settings. Missing `gh`, logged out or auth failure = feed off, status in Settings. Polls follow `X-Poll-Interval` (min 60 s) with `If-Modified-Since` and back off on errors.
- **Pill (D7) and hotkey (D8):** top item = highest-severity attention item, oldest first; with none, the pill shows the most recently updated running item and hides when nothing is live. Pill content: source · state · elapsed, plus the title truncated to one line; clicking it opens the panel on that item. It sits below the notch on notched displays, below the menu bar centre otherwise, on the menu-bar display; it never takes focus, joins all Spaces, and in full-screen Spaces shows only while an attention item exists. Hotkey: Carbon `RegisterEventHotKey` (no Accessibility permission), no default, set in Settings with an inline conflict error; it opens the panel on the top item, or the first Live item when Needs you is empty. In the panel, Return = Open (D10 rules), Delete = Dismiss, arrows move, S = Snooze menu.
- **Snooze (D10):** `snoozedUntil` (see activity model) is set from a row's Snooze menu or `attn snooze <id> 15m|1h|tomorrow`. A snoozed item has no attention, isn't counted in N, has no ring segment and no banner; snoozing removes its delivered banner. It shows in Live with a clock. "Tomorrow" = the next 09:00 local after now. When `snoozedUntil` passes (on wake if it passed during sleep), attention rises again as a new episode with a banner (an explicit exception to D9's push rule) unless the item was resolved, expired or dismissed. A push that changes state while snoozed clears the snooze and applies normally (`done` → Recent); a same-state push only refreshes text and heartbeat and leaves the snooze in place. `snoozedUntil` is persisted, wall-clock.
- **Launch and writes (CEO Section 2, rescue for approved behaviour):** on launch, the app tries to connect to an existing socket; if that fails it unlinks the stale file and binds, and if it succeeds another instance is running, so it focuses that instance and quits. `attn setup claude` writes settings.json to a temp file and renames it into place, so a failed write never leaves a half-written file.
- **Store format (re-review D4, supersedes CEO D12):** the store file carries a schema version. Known older versions are migrated in place, with one migration and test per schema change. An unknown or corrupt file is moved aside (kept), the app starts empty, and Recent shows "Saved items couldn't be read after the update; open requests may be missing".
- **Update security and logging (CEO Sections 3, 8; mechanics of approved D6 and behaviour):** Sparkle updates are EdDSA-signed and the appcast is served over HTTPS. The app logs with `os_log` under one subsystem, with categories `ipc`, `store`, `github`, `hooks` and `ui`.
- **Sources are a common, pluggable list (user requirement, 2026-10-30):** many more providers are planned, so every provider (Claude Code, GitHub, and later Codex, Calendar, the Notification Centre store and others) is added, removed and shown through one shared Sources UI: a list with + / − buttons and an "Add a source" picker, used both in first run and in Settings. The picker opens as a sheet from the window's title bar and stays open through a provider's own setup steps, such as Claude Code's settings.json diff or a "sign in to gh first" hint (design review 7A). Each provider has a name, an icon, a status line and its own setup/teardown steps behind the same Add and Remove actions.
- **Provider architecture (eng review 3, D1 = A):** providers are built into the app behind one Swift protocol, kept in a registry. The Sources UI, `attn doctor` and the store only ever use this protocol:
  ```
  protocol Provider {
    id, displayName, icon, summary          // shown in the Add a source sheet
    availability: available | comingSoon
    status: ProviderStatus                  // .working(detail) | .needsSetup(reason, fix) | .error(message, retry) | .off
    setupSteps() -> [SetupStep]             // shown inside the sheet (e.g. settings.json diff, "sign in to gh")
    remove()                                // undoes its own setup (e.g. attn setup claude --remove)
    start() / stop()                        // feeds the activity store with source = id
  }
  ```
  v1 providers: Claude Code (hooks), GitHub (gh feed), and "any app" (the attn command: every distinct `source` that pushes becomes its own row). Later providers (Codex, Calendar, the Notification Centre store) are new types in the same registry. There is no plugin system; outside apps integrate through `attn`.
  - **Provider type vs source identity (eng review 3, outside voice #1):** a provider type (e.g. `claude`, `github`, `app`) is separate from a source id. Built-in providers own their reserved ids. Every `attn push` source is namespaced as `app:<name>`, and a push naming a reserved id is rejected with a clear error, so a script can never share a built-in provider's items or be removed with it.
  - **Removal lifecycle (eng review 3, outside voice #2):** − runs these steps in order: `stop()`; mark the source removed, so the store rejects any new event and any in-flight callback (such as a GitHub poll) for it; run `remove()`; clear its items. If `remove()` fails, the row stays visible in Error ("Couldn't remove — Retry") and ingestion stays blocked until a retry succeeds.
  - **Removing a source (eng review 3, D2):** − runs the provider's `remove()` and also clears that source's items from Needs you, Live and the ring. A confirmation gives the count first ("Remove GitHub and its 3 items?").
  - **Removing an "any app" source (eng review 3, D3):** the app didn't install it, so − mutes it. The source id is remembered and its pushes are ignored; `attn push` from a muted source exits 0 quietly, so scripts don't break. The row moves to a "Muted" group with an Unmute button. Its existing items are cleared as in D2.

- **Empty states (design review 3A):** *No sources:* the ring is an empty outline, and the panel shows "Connect a source to start" with an Add a source… button that opens the shared picker. *All clear:* the panel shows "Nothing needs you", then "Watching N sources · last event 4m ago", and Recent stays available below. **Activity model:**

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `source`, `id` | Identity. A push with the same pair replaces the previous one |
| `revision` | Optional. If the sender supplies one, a push at or below the stored revision is dropped. If omitted, the app uses arrival order, so stateless shell hooks just push |
| `state` | `running` / `waiting` / `done` / `error` / `unknown` |
| `attention` | Boolean, separate from state. `ack` clears it only if the item's app-assigned revision still equals the one displayed when the click or `attn ack` was issued; a failed Open leaves it set with a row error (eng review D10). Attention rises only on a transition into `waiting`/`error`, or on a push with a newer sender revision; a same-state repeat refreshes the heartbeat and text only (eng review D9) |
| `severity` | `info` / `normal` (default) / `high` / `critical`, set by the sender |
| `expiresAt` | Optional, set by the sender. The item moves to Recent at that time |
| `open` | A URL only. Opened with `NSWorkspace.open`, never through a shell. Any scheme with an installed handler app (checked at push with `NSWorkspace.urlForApplication(toOpen:)`); unhandled schemes are rejected at push time. Hover shows the handler app (eng review D7) |
| `progress` | Optional 0-1, drawn as a ring segment or bar |
| `snoozedUntil` | Optional wall-clock time; while set the item has no attention, no ring segment and isn't counted (CEO D10) |
| `ttl` | Heartbeat timeout for `running` items. App-owned default 10 min; a sender may override per push |

**State from each sender:**

| Sender | `running` (+ heartbeat refresh) | `waiting` + attention | `done` | `error` + attention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GitHub notifications feed — v1 (CEO D5) | — | review requested, mention, assign | thread read or marked done on GitHub → `resolve` | CI failure (`ci_activity`) |
| Claude Code hooks — v1 (CEO D1) | `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse` (each sets `running` and refreshes the heartbeat) | `Notification` (ignored if the item is `done`: that's the idle reminder) | `Stop`; `SessionEnd` → `resolve` | `StopFailure` (re-review D5) |
| EventKit — follow-up (D2) | — | — | event start → expiry | — |

**Transitions on later pushes:**
- A `running` push (e.g. the next `PreToolUse` after you answered) on a `waiting` item sets `running` and clears `attention`, because the agent got what it needed.
- A `done` push clears `attention` and moves the item to Recent.
- An `error` keeps attention until a newer `running` push, a `resolve`, an Open/ack or a Dismiss.

**One authority per session:** each agent session has one sender: its own hooks. Claude Code hooks key items on the Claude Code session id. Codex's `notify` program is the likely equivalent; its payload fields (and whether it reports anything besides turn completion) are unverified. The app is standalone and depends on no other app.

Heartbeat only applies to `running` items. A hook-only agent stuck in one long tool call longer than its `ttl` shows `unknown`. That's correct: the tool can't know it's still alive. Silence is measured on `SuspendingClock`, which pauses while the Mac sleeps, so sleep never causes a false `unknown` and nothing is reset. Items already `unknown` stay `unknown` across wake and relaunch; after a relaunch, silence is the wall time since each item's saved last heartbeat (eng review D11).

**Where items show and how they leave:**

| Item | Panel section | Dock ring | Counted in N |
|---|---|---|---|
| `attention` = true (waiting / error) | Needs you | Solid segment | Yes |
| `running`, no attention | Live | Outline segment | No |
| `waiting` / `error`, attention cleared by Open/ack | Live, labelled "waiting · seen" or "error · seen" | Outline segment with a gap mark | No |
| `unknown` | Live, marked "no update in Xm" with a Dismiss button | Dashed segment | No |
| `done`, or `info` without attention | Recent notices | None | No |
| snoozed (`snoozedUntil` set) | Live, with a clock | None | No |

- **Open** opens the `open` URL and acks the item if the revision shown is still current; if the URL fails to open, the item stays lit with an error (D10).
- **Dismiss** (the panel's "Done" button, renamed so it doesn't clash with the state) moves any item to Recent. It's the exit for `unknown` items and for items with no sender expiry.
- **`resolve`** from the source moves the item to Recent and keeps a tombstone of its revision for 24h, so a delayed older push can't bring it back. A revisionless push after `resolve` is treated as a new run. That's how a hook's next `UserPromptSubmit` starts a fresh activity.
- **Recent notices** is history, not attention: the last 50 items or 24h, whichever is smaller. It never counts toward N and never drives the Dock.

**Severity rendering:** within a section, items sort by severity (critical first), then oldest first. The **top item** (its initial sits in the ring centre) is the highest-severity attention item, oldest first. On the ring, `critical` uses the red system colour and a "!" centre, `high` uses orange, and `normal` uses the accent colour. `info` items never get a segment.

**The count N:** the menu bar's `◉ N` is the number of items with `attention` = true. It's hidden at 0 (the slot keeps its width) and shows `9+` above 9. The ring shows at most 6 segments, and anything beyond collapses into one "+N" segment.

**Clearing rules (from Q3):** clicked/opened (`ack`), source says resolved (`resolve`), sender-set expiry, plus an explicit Dismiss for anything the source never resolves. No inference from app activity and no global auto-expiry of attention items, by choice.

**Socket security:** the socket sits in `~/Library/Application Support/Attn/` (directory 0700, socket 0600). The app checks the connecting process's uid with `getpeereid` and rejects any uid other than the user's. Combined with the URL-only `open` field, a local process can at worst add an item whose click opens a URL in an installed app, which it could already do itself as the same user. It can never run a shell command.

**App shell:** a regular-activation-policy app, *not* `LSUIElement`, because an `LSUIElement` app has no Dock tile. It has a Dock icon (kept in the Dock, launched as a login item) plus an `NSStatusItem` in the menu bar. If you quit it, both surfaces disappear and senders get a non-zero exit from `attn`.

**UI** (the office-hours wireframe at `designs/sketch-20261028/` is superseded by the approved mockups in the design review's "Approved Mockups" section; where they differ, the plan text wins: no severity stripe, picker as a sheet):
- **Dock ring:** one segment per live, non-snoozed activity. Outline = running, solid = needs you (with the top item's initial in the centre), dashed = unknown. No numeric badge.
- **Menu bar:** a fixed-width `◉ N` item that never resizes on state change (prior learning: no layout shift). The panel always shows sections in this order: Needs you → Live → Recent notices (collapsed). A footer says chat still goes to Notification Centre.

## Success Criteria
